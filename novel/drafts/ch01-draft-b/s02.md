**Nowhere. No time.**

I come to on my back with grit between my teeth.

There's no sand on the cherry road, so before I open my eyes I already know I'm not on it. I lie there and check my body one piece at a time. Toes. Knees. The ribs, which should be flat. The spine, which should be a story doctors tell each other later.

Everything answers. Nothing hurts. I'd have preferred pain. Pain means a hospital.

The sky has no colour. It's the inside of your eyelids, without a sun or a seam. Under it, to the horizon, there are dunes.

I pick up a handful of the sand and let it run, and halfway through I stop, because it isn't sand.

I'm holding a hairpin. A baby tooth. A key for a lock that's long since been changed. The whole dune is made of things, worn down to grains, and bigger things poke out of it the way bones do in a real desert. A bicycle with the chain off. A dog's leash with no dog. A school bag with a name tag the weather has taken. A letter folded small enough to hide in a fist, never opened, or never sent.

Close by my foot, half sunk and still wet, is a low branch with pink on it.

I stepped over that branch this morning.

In a dip just below me a wedding ring catches the no-light, and while I'm looking at it, it goes. It doesn't sink. It fades, like a photograph left in a shop window, until there's only the dip.

Three metres off, half buried, is a pair of reading glasses with the left arm mended with tape.

I know that tape. I did it. I was eleven and he wore them taped for four years out of a stubbornness I've since recognised in myself.

I don't pick them up. I'm afraid they'll fade while I'm holding them.

***

"You died, Kael."

He's sitting on the crest of the next dune, where a second ago there was only dune. I say *he*. He's tall and narrow and wrapped in something pale, and he's made of dust, the kind that turns in a shaft of sun through a dirty window, gold and never still. There's a face. It keeps almost arriving.

His voice is dry and not loud. Pages turning in a library after closing.

"The tree was older than you. Roots like that go a long way. It fell. That's all it was."

"Who are you?"

"Whoever you like. People have been very creative." A hand, or the idea of one, takes in the dunes. "This is mine. Everything that's finished. Somebody has to keep it."

"The past."

"If you need a job title." He follows my eye to the ring-shaped dip. "That's what it looks like, from this side, when the last person who remembers something stops."

I should be screaming. There's a part of me doing it, a long way down. But the part on top sat in a police station at four in the morning answering questions about accelerants while a woman in a cardigan kept offering me a juice box, and learned that if you go cold enough you can get through nearly anything, and pay for it later.

So I go cold.

"If I'm dead, why are we talking?"

"Because you asked me for something. You stood on my step and said it out loud." He tilts his almost-head. "The little house under the tree. The old woman sweeps it."

The shrine. The circle with the line through it.

"I wasn't talking to you. People *say* that."

"*I wish I was ten again. Things were so much simpler.*" He gives it back in my own voice, and it sounds like whining. "You said it on my step at an hour when I happened to be listening. I've decided to say yes."

"You can't."

"Then you should be more careful where you stand."

There's an hourglass in his hand that wasn't there before. Clouded glass, a frame of dark wood worn pale at the corners, and sand inside the same gold as him.

"You'll be a child. Ten, since you were specific. Somewhere that's finished; I'll choose where. Two years." He turns it over, and a grain lets go with a sound I've never heard anywhere else. *Tick.* "Two floods of the river. When the second one goes down, I take my hand off you, and you go back exactly where you were."

*Seven hundred and thirty days,* some part of me counts, because counting is what that part does when it's frightened.

On my back under eighty years of cherry tree, with my phone going.

"You're lending me two years and then sending me back to die."

"You're already dead. That part's done." He says it kindly. I don't forgive him for that. "I'm lending you some time I happen to have. It isn't really mine to lend. Try not to waste it."

"And if I don't want it."

He doesn't answer. The sand keeps falling.

***

"Then tell me the rules."

My hands have started to shake. I put them under my arms so he won't see.

"They'll understand you, and you'll understand them. It'll feel like talking through a wall, and they'll hear the wall." He counts it off on dust fingers. "You keep what you carried nearest your heart. Nothing else."

"That's it?"

"That's a great deal."

"Why me?"

He goes quiet. Behind me a dune sighs, a thousand small forgotten things settling. He looks past me, down the slope, at the glasses with the taped arm.

Then he says, in my mother's voice, exactly, the pitch and the catch in it and all:

"*Don't let them forget his name.*"

I'm on my feet. I don't remember getting there.

"She said it to me first," he says, in his own voice. "On the grass. She was more polite about it than you've been."

"Whose name? *Whose?* What did she—"

"Simple?" the god says, and the dust finally manages a mouth, and it's smiling. "We shall see."

The dune behind him lifts. A wall of gold grains rising into that colourless sky and bending toward me, and I get one breath, and it's full of other people's lost things.

Then it's on me, and I'm going down, and I'm going *in*. My sleeves swallow my hands. Something is folding me up like a letter, shorter and shorter, and the blazer is a tent and then it isn't anywhere, and the last sound is small and patient and very close to my ear.

*Tick.*

Light. White, flat, enormous light, and heat with it, and a smell so thick it has texture.

Fish. Smoke. River.
