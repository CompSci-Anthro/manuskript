# Throne of Ashes — Story Bible
<!-- Cap: 1,500 words. Read every session. Terse fragments, not prose. -->

## Premise
- Logline: Kael, 18, dies under a falling cherry tree in Japan after wishing to be ten again. The God of the Past grants it his way: two floods of the Nile in Tutankhamun's Egypt, in a ten-year-old's body, then back under the tree to die. Arrested as a foreign spy, he is sentenced by the nine-year-old king to be his "royal playmate and pet," and must survive a court that kills by whisper.
- Genre / tone: historical light novel, political thriller. Tense, serious, mature. A little horror, lots of suspense, no comedy chapters. Stakes explode to civil war by the end of Vol 1.
- Target length: Vol 1 = 10 chapters (6,500–10,000 words each) + epilogue "What the Sand Remembers"; series 10–20 vols. Vol 1 arc: "The Cursed Playmate."
- Hidden mystery (don't answer in Vol 1): why the god chose Kael; the arson; his mother's last words, repeated by the god in her voice: "Don't let them forget his name."
- Series end (planning only): Tut is assassinated; Ay takes the throne as history records; Kael stays the unseen hand.

## Cast
<!-- Name — role; look (1 line); voice/tics; wants; secret -->
- **Kael** — hero, narrator. 18 in a 10-year-old's body; 130 cm, slim, fair, wheat-blond hair in rough layers over his forehead, big round blue eyes, thin white scar through his left eyebrow (snapped foil, age 12). Mother's woven cord, red gone to rust, on his left wrist, never removed. The god's hourglass hidden in the knot of his kilt; gold grains let go one at a time. Servant's faded indigo kilt knotted twice, plain sandals. Voice high and bright; speaks formal, precise, archaic ("like a scribe's grandfather"), faint foreign lilt; never swears; sharpest tongue; talks the most. Inner voice darker and colder: a ledger of debts and betrayals, a page for everyone who decided his life without asking. Mantra: *They see a child.* Habits: counts steps, maps the palace in the sand under his mat and erases it at dawn, picks loose threads, eats nothing he hasn't watched poured or tasted, insomniac. Skills: nine years of fencing, stealth, lockpicking, reading faces; body now weak. Cover story: raised by herders far to the east, robbed on the road. Never reveals age/origin, never harms Tut, never abandons Kalici, never begs. Knowledge: historian parents (chart of kings over his bed, museum trips); knows Tut dies at nineteen, Nakhtmin's shabtis, Ay, Horemheb, the Restoration Stela. Loves myrrh. Flaws: arrogant, lies easily, can't ask for help. Craves Tut's approval and hides it.
- **Tutankhamun (Tut)** — partner. 9, pharaoh, throne name Nebkheperure, renamed from Tutankhaten early (the Vizier's doing). Slight, fine-boned, tan, black hair under the nemes; gold collar; obsidian scarab from his father, touched when afraid; fidgets with his collar; thumbnail bitten to the quick. Few flat words; forgets to be king when playing; calls himself "Pharaoh" when angry. Freezes in conflict, but braver than his advisors think; senses manipulation. Loves senet, roasted quail eggs with cumin and salt, sketching temples; a small carved wooden boat hidden in his linens. Father (the old king) was poisoned (Kael learns Ch4). Private name for Kael later: "Khenti." Told Kael: "Learn slowly."
- **Aneb** — rival, later ally. 18, palace slave, Tut's since the king was small; "the Pharaoh's Shadow." 185 cm, lean, tan, black wavy hair parted right, hazel eyes, pale burn scar diagonally across his face (the raid that burned his village and family). Plain undyed linen. Low calm voice, few words; interrupts Kael, calls him "Boy," curt only with Kael, gentle with everyone else, mild swearing, slang. Tastes every dish for Tut (since the king was four). Stealth, sneaking up on people; paces when nervous; prays to Amun at night. Line: "Even pharaohs bleed sand. Somebody has to watch the door." Rule: serve the king first.
- **Nakhti** — mentor. 40, royal advisor, "the Raven of Kemet"; found by the old king in a slave gang on a canal, raised up for his mind; the court despises him for it. Tall, broad, slightly stooped, gray early, hair tied in a soldier's tail, left eye scarred shut, right eye green, a tattoo, ink-stained fingers, fine white linen, leather sash with herbs and papyrus, bare feet, one copper ring. A bronze dagger with a cracked blade hangs on his wall. Smooth, formal, long pauses; answers questions with questions. Sayings: "The Nile remembers what men forget." / "The gods whisper to those who listen. Wisdom hides in silence, not in shouting." Weakness: wine, from mid-afternoon. His price for teaching Kael: never lie to him (Kael already does). Regret: his silence during a palace coup cost the old king's heir. Secret: a list of everyone at the old king's last table (Ch5). Fate: framed and arrested in the epilogue.
- **Amenhotep** — villain. 45, Vizier, "the Whisperer." Tallest and thinnest man in any room; gaunt angular face, three ritual scars on each cheekbone, hazel eyes that go gold in lamplight, shaved head with one gold band, indigo robes with gold Amun glyphs, lapis-and-carnelian collar, falcon-headed staff, hands wrapped in linen, one thumbnail black to the root. Deep smooth voice, long pauses, never raised; threats sound like kindness. "Order before mercy." Never speaks to Kael in public; marked him the first night; in Ch2 smiled at him across the hall. Plays senet alone with a cheap piece. Never harms the king openly; always has a scapegoat; never dirties his hands. Keeps Kael's ¥10,000 bill in a cedar box. His people: his steward (soft hands), the hooded man (myrrh and burnt honey), the split-lipped cup servant, guards, scribes, informants. Poisoned the old king (planning only).
- **Nakhtmin** — Commander of the Royal Retainers, Shield of the Living Pharaoh. 22, broad-shouldered, scarred jaw, black hair pulled back severely, eyes far older than he is; blood red. Three hundred men. Reports to the Vizier. Speaks only in orders and reports; ends every accepted order with "It will be done." Sketches formations in dust. Cut Kael's rope in Ch1; cut the assassin's throat in Ch2.
- **Wennefer** — High Priest of Amun. NOT NAMED on the page until Ch8 ("the High Priest"). 32, lean, shaved head, leopard skin over one shoulder, counts breaths between words. Looks to the Vizier before he acts.
- **Userhat** — fat treasury official who demanded Kael's death in Ch1.
- **Hori** — the soldier who took Kael from the river, whispered the court's names, left bread: "That one lasted eleven days." "I was told to." Kind or ordered: unknown.
- **Kanefer** — councilor, Overseer of the Granaries of the North; poisoned at dinner in Ch2 for asking about the temple's grain. Young widow.
- Later: **Kalici** (servant girl, Ch3), **Ren** (masked stranger, Ch9). Japan: twin **Soren** (with their uncle in Osaka), **Mrs. Hayashi** (six books unpaid), **Henut** the baker.

## World rules
- Only supernatural force: the God of the Past and the curse. No powers. Kael wins by mind and sacrifice, never strength. Every death matters; none undone.
- Deadline: two floods. When the second goes down, back under the tree. Ch1 = day 1, "seven hundred and twenty-nine to go." Kael tallies days on his wall beside the last boy's ten and a half.
- Kael understands and is understood, but every word reaches him half a beat late.
- Hard law: no execution without the Weighing of the Heart before the pharaoh; the Vizier cannot skip the rite.
- History wins: real events stay accurate, the story lives in the gaps. About the third year of Tut's reign. Hatti is taking cities in the north (Djahy). Horemheb commands the northern army, two years away. Ay, God's Father, keeps silent at Akhmim. Real names spelled the common English way; invented Egyptians get short names from real parts (Nakht-, -min, Wen-, Ka-). Kael's is the only foreign-sounding name.
- Palace: west bank of Thebes. Throne room: lotus columns, gold-leafed ceiling, myrrh, a throne too big for Tut, a hammered-out cartouche behind it. Tut's bedchamber: painted marsh of reeds and birds (one bird now red), bed on lion's feet, heavy curtains over the far wall hiding a plain servants' door, senet board by the bed, the carved boat under the linens, a ceiling vent with a bronze grate (loosened; never say so yet), two door guards. Kael's room: 212 steps and four turns from Tut's, narrow, bar across the door, window slit, reed mat; sandal under the mat; maps in the sand. House of Life (archive): second court past the kitchens; Nakhti's room three turns beyond it. Dining hall: long, columned, hundreds of lamps, lotus bowls, three harpists, the king's raised seat with a footrest he can't reach, Nakhti at the lowest table by the door.
- Power: the temple of Amun holds a third of the fields; the Vizier administers the king's; Nakhtmin's guards are the only blades inside the walls that aren't the Vizier's.
- Terms (exact spelling): Kemet, deben, Ma'at, Duat, senet, ka, ba, uraeus, khopesh, Vizier, Medjay, Khasut ("sand-foreign"). Greeting "May the Nile guide your steps." Blessing "May Amun keep your name." Proverb "The Nile remembers what men forget." Wonder: "The gods have opened their eyes." Disaster: "Ammit take it." Insult: "shadow-toucher." Vizier's faction: "Order before mercy." Team oath (later): "What flows returns." Court's name for Kael: "the Pharaoh's Toy."
- Factions: the Vizier's (seal, treasury, scribes, Medjay, the priesthood of Amun leaning his way) holds the grain. The Pharaoh's: Tut, Aneb, Nakhti, Nakhtmin's guards (orders from the other side), a few old servants, and now Kael.

## Style sheet
- POV / tense: first person, present tense, Kael. Close, but shows things he misses. Modern words allowed. Memories of Japan flash in.
- Paragraphs 1–3 sentences, often one line; new line per dialogue beat. Dialogue about 40%, description rich, teen reading level. Lyrical but cold: Nile mud, incense, sweat.
- Thoughts in *italics*. Key lines on their own line. Pauses and stutters allowed. No "……" silence lines; use an action beat.
- American spelling (color, gray, meters). Double quotes. "Mrs."
- Violence slow, vivid and painful. Rating mature. Never: sexual or romantic content involving anyone in a child's body; male-male romance; real-world politics; preaching; shouted technique names; slurs.
- Every chapter ends on a cliffhanger or reveal and has at least one emotional beat. Comedy optional. Time jumps: scene break with a time note. Scene break: ***. Scene headers: "## N · Title"; chapter title as "# Chapter N — Title" at the top of s01.
- Address: Tut is "Majesty" or "Pharaoh"; the Vizier is "my lord"; Kael is "boy," "the king's pet." Tut says "Pharaoh" of himself when angry.
- Voices: Kael formal and precise; Tut few flat words; Amenhotep soft, elegant, long pauses; Nakhti questions and metaphors; Nakhtmin orders and reports; Aneb curt and slangy to Kael only.
