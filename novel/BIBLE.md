# Throne of Ashes — Story Bible

## Premise
- Logline: Kael, 18, dies in Japan and wakes in Tutankhamun's Thebes cursed into a 10-year-old's body. Made the boy king's playmate, he must outwit the Vizier's court with a mind no one suspects.
- Clock: two years. If he fails, he's pulled back into his own corpse in the present.
- Genre: dark historical drama (50%) + romance/intimacy (35%) + mystery (15%). Rare fights. 18+ readers who like Red River.
- Tone: dark, tense, bittersweet. Serious (10/10). "Beautiful but dangerous." Opens as the calm before the storm and ends ominous. Moments of silence and beauty. Mood color: dried-blood red. Sound: a gong.
- Volume 1, "The Cursed Playmate": 9 chapters + flash-forward opening + epilogue. ~70k words. Chapters run 5–8k words, 3–5 scenes each. About a month passes. Series is planned at 10–20 volumes.
- Title's secret meaning: Throne of Ashes is Kael's original future (death in two years), which he must burn down by reshaping Egypt from within.

## Cast
- **Kael Amunet** (hero, narrator). 18 inside, 10-year-old body. Japanese; in Japan he had black hair and a black school uniform. After the curse he's small and soft-featured with blond hair (Kazuto-style cut). His history-professor parents died in a fire, and their lessons are his "modern knowledge" (facts remembered, not powers). Strategist. Polite, formal, clipped and precise, with archaic phrasing that unnerves people; never swears. Overused thought: "They see a child." Catchphrase: "The gods don't whisper to fools." When scared he gives a bitter laugh: "Just another variable I didn't account for." Wants control over his fate. Needs someone to see the real him. Fears exposure, and secretly fears the child body will dull his mind. Loves Tut. Hates powerlessness. Arc type: corruption, as he crosses lines to survive and learns that bonds matter more than pure manipulation. Nickname from generals: "the Pharaoh's Toy."
- **Tutankhamun** ("Tut"; the partner). 9, newly crowned, with no real power. Bright high voice, few words, cryptic and lonely. Smarter than he shows. Catchphrase: "The gods whisper what mortals refuse to hear." Names Kael "My playmate" in Ch1. In Vol 1 he defies his advisors' war orders, guided by Kael's quiet counsel. Kael would die for him.
- **Amenhotep the Vizier** ("the Whisperer"; villain). 45. Calm, cruel, charming, polite. Deep smooth baritone; never raises his voice. Laugh: a low chuckle cut off short. Collects carved scarabs (one per conquered enemy). Plays senet alone with a cheap piece. Eats honey figs alone. Motive: power 40, greed 35, ideology 25. Calls foreigners "khat-su" and despises them. Rules: never harms the pharaoh openly, always has a scapegoat ready, never dirties his own hands. Group motto: "Order before mercy." Catchphrase: "The child's eyes betray him." Someone bigger stands behind him (later volumes).
- **Aneb** (rival). 18, palace slave who has served Tut since infancy; "the Pharaoh's Shadow." Low, calm, few words. Devoted, jealous, stealthy. "A slave I may be, I am human." Catchphrase: "Even pharaohs bleed sand." Shifts loyalty toward Kael. Ch8: helps hide the body without a word.
- **Nakhti** (mentor). 40, former slave turned royal advisor and temple scribe; "the Raven of Kemet." Weathered, cryptic, faithful. Smooth formal voice with long pauses. Teaches survival and masking with strict, hands-on lessons, and grows to love Kael like a son. "The gods whisper to those who listen—wisdom hides in silence, not in shouting." Framed for the steward's death and arrested at the end of Vol 1.
- **Kalici** (emotional anchor; not a romance). 10, Kael's hand servant. Soft voice that rambles. Perceptive; sees through masks: "What mask are you hiding today?" The Vizier sent her to frame Kael with bruises given by his steward, but she couldn't scream, and Kael asked who hurt her. Becomes his double agent in the Vizier's house. POV for Ch7.
- **Ren** (wildcard). Masked: raven-black leather, golden mask, carved bronze ring. Rough gravelly voice, few words. Appears once (Ch9), drops through the ceiling vent and saves Kael. "I do not care about the pharaoh. I have been tasked to save you, Kael." Says Kael isn't the first outsider he's saved. Also, unseen, frees Wennefer in Ch8.
- **Nakhtmin**. 22, Commander of the Royal Retainers and Shield of the Living Pharaoh. Scarred jaw, black hair pulled back severely, eyes older than his age. Cold and flat: "It will be done." Sketches battle formations in the dust. Officially serves under Amenhotep; becomes the team's spy inside the Vizier's camp. Gets hurt.
- **Wennefer**. 32, High Priest of Amun. Silver-streaked braids, lapis and gold. Speaks in measured, breath-counted pauses. Suspects the curse is divine punishment, gets a partial truth from Kael in Ch8, is tortured, freed by a masked stranger. Keeps Kael's secret to the grave.
- **The God of the Past**. Grants Kael's dying wish ("I wish I was a kid again") "in his own way." A void of ancient sundials. "Simple? We shall see." Appears only in Ch1 and the epilogue's last image.
- **The hooded man**. Serves the Vizier, smells of myrrh and burnt honey, never named in Vol 1.
- **The steward**. Amenhotep's man, who bruised Kalici; the Ch9 assassin, killed by Ren.
- Minor: Mrs. Hayashi (tea shop, Ch1 opening).

## World rules
- Thebes, Kemet, during Tutankhamun's early reign. History always wins: real events stay accurate and the story lives in the gaps. Tut's real death (1323 BCE) is fixed, and Ay succeeds him.
- No magic or powers. The only supernatural force is the God of the Past and the curse. Kael wins with his mind and with sacrifice, never strength. No overpowered hero.
- No execution until the Weighing of the Heart is performed before the pharaoh.
- Assassination attempts on the king are routine (Ch2). The old pharaoh was poisoned.
- Kael's 10,000-yen bill is kept as evidence (Amenhotep studies it in the epilogue).
- Inner-circle oath: the Nile Pact (water on the hands). Group phrase: "What flows returns."
- Slang: greeting "May the Nile guide your steps." Blessing "May Amun keep your name." Curses "Ammit take it" / "By the Duat's teeth!" Proverb "The Nile remembers what men forget." Battle cry "For Ma'at and the Two Lands!" Insult "shadow-toucher." Outsiders are called "khat-su." Money: deben.
- Names follow common English spellings (Tutankhamun, Amenhotep). Patronymics ("X-son-of-Y"). Honorifics: Master, Pharaoh, Egyptian titles.

## Style sheet
- First person, Kael, **present tense**. Narration in his 18-year-old voice: modern, sharp, cold and precise, keeping a ledger of debts and betrayals. Lyrical but cold, full of sensory detail (Nile mud, incense, sweat). Close to his thoughts, but showing things he misses. Constant awareness of child body vs adult mind.
- Mix: dialogue 40, action 25, narration 25, inner thoughts 10. Paragraphs often a single line. Easy reading level. Clean and quick.
- Dialogue: dramatic but realistic. Kael speaks short, clipped and precise, which sets him apart from Tut (both have high voices). Mild swearing, in real words. No Japanese words.
- Invented and Egyptian terms are explained through context only. Modern units (meters, hours).
- Violence reads slow, vivid and painful, shown directly. Death is common and harsh, and bystanders can die on the page.
- Every chapter ends on a cliffhanger. Scene headers only when the place or time jumps (flashbacks too). Long, descriptive light-novel chapter titles. A POV switch gets its own chapter or interlude. Amenhotep interludes appear only at the opening of Ch3 and the start and end of Ch6.
- Hard limits: no Boys' Love. Romance involving adults is kept off the page. No romance at all involving the children (Kalici, Tut, Kael's child body). Avoid real-world politics.
- Tone-killers to avoid: jokes during sad scenes, edginess for its own sake, constant sarcasm.
- Narration examples: "The Nile gave Kemet its mud and its mercy, and I gave it a child's face and a grown man's lies." / "They see a child's face and assume a child's mind. Let them."
- Fixed lines: the Vol 1 first line "My parents were obsessed with history. I wish I was ten again. Life was so much simpler." The first Egypt narration line "The Nile smells of fish, myrrh and rot, and in the market of Thebes I am ten years old again…" The final line "They say gods don't make mistakes. Tonight I decided to become the one god of this age that Egypt can't ignore, child's face and all."
